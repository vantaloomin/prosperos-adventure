# Changelog

## Unreleased

- **Author and Watch play, and switching between modes.** In Author, a nudge sent with Next goes to the director and the Writer as a hidden note, and the line under the nudge box says after the turn whether it took ("Used: Mara took it up.", "Not yet: no one took it up this turn."); the beat board is the director's plan for each arc, where the Author adds, cuts and reorders beats and sees which have landed. In Play, beats you would like to see (a storm, a stranger arriving) can wait beside your line and go to the director the same way. Watch plays turns on your timer and pauses at deaths, betrayals, chapter ends, a new nemesis and the session cap, with Continue to go on; Next steps by hand. Outside Play your character runs on an agent or its Program mind, your pick, and leaving Play asks whether to see the motives you missed, hidden unless you say so. (Backend; the screens come with the play screen work.)
- **The GM view asks first while characters think.** In Play with a character who thinks, opening the GM view asks "This shows every secret and motive. Open anyway?", with "Don't ask again in this story". Once open, the GM view lists each mind's intent, lines, whispers and motive for the turn. A campaign where nobody thinks opens it as before.
- **The last turn's report.** Between turns, under the round timeline, the last turn's number opens its report in place: what you set, each roll in plain words (with the number when rolls are shown), the motives the mode lets you see, what the story made up that turn (each with Strike, which takes it out and rewrites the passage), the checks' rewrites, open questions, corrections, and where the turn stopped.
- **One Writer for a round with characters who think.** When agents share the scene, the round is written once from everything it settled: what each character said and did, every roll as the story feels it (never the dice), the beats you set in full, and the cutaways to people off the page, stopping before your character's next real choice. In Play it stays close on your character; in Author and Watch it roves across the cast, and each Author or Watch turn now leaves its prose in the story. A campaign with no agents is told exactly as before.
- **Pace in Settings > Models.** Quick, Balanced or Rich for the characters who think, each with its calls and tokens a turn. Advanced holds how many think at once, exchanges a turn, how readily they scheme, how fast loyalties shift, and a token cap for the session with what it has spent so far.
- **Characters who think for themselves, on the play screen.** Open a character in who's here to choose Thinks (an agent) or Rules (Dungeon's code minds); a character who thinks has an accent dot on their portrait, and the round timeline (Declare, React, Talk, Roll, Write) sits above the cast, lighting and naming the step under way ("Declaring · 2 of 3"). A row says only that a character has decided, never what. When a character speaks to yours, the message box gives way to a reply card with their line and the two before it: Reply, Say nothing, or Let your character answer for that one line. The card comes back after a reload, the tab's title shows (1) while it waits, and screen readers hear "2 of 3 characters have decided". A campaign where nobody thinks looks and plays as before.
- **Lore that not everyone knows.** A lore entry can be public, known to some characters, or GM only, and the narrator reads only public lore. Untagged lore stays public. A world package's hidden truths and its cast's secrets count as GM only or known to the characters they belong to, never public, and the world importer names any visibility tag it cannot read.
- **Agents in Settings > Models.** A new Agents role picks the model the agent characters think with (the Default without one), and a character can have a model of its own. The agents' preset (Quick, Balanced, Rich, each with an estimate of calls and tokens a turn), how much they scheme, how fast their loyalties shift and a spend cap for the session are kept per campaign, ready for the cast and settings screens.
- **World pitches on hover.** Hovering or focusing a world on the title screen floats its pitch over the art in a frosted card above the name, which stays put.
- **Plainer model settings.** Models, Decisions and Recall are each a heading and an Add button, without the explanations above them. The computer report shows only when a chosen local model won't fit, and the job picker is called Roles.
- **A calmer play screen.** The scene is one line: the place (tap it to go somewhere else), the time and how you are. The composer is the message box and Send; Ideas, the author's note, stepping out of the story, every panel view and starting a new story are in a menu (the button beside the box, or ⌘K / Ctrl+K). The panel opens on who's here: you first, then the people met at this place, each opening to show more, with who else is around and the weather under them. The Scene, Sheet, Pack, Journal and More tabs are gone. "Characters are AI and can make mistakes" stays under the box.
- **One cast page.** "Who are you?" and "How this campaign plays" are one page: the town's people as a grid, tap one to play them and read who they are, or + to make your own; Rules, Tone (As written, Gentler, Harsher) and Begin at the foot. Ironman, an earlier story and how far you can rise sit behind Options; the finer tone, narration and dice choices start from the preset and your last campaign and change in play under Tone and rules.
- **A quieter start.** The first-run notice is one line over the title art ("Everyone here is AI"), the 18-or-older box, a Safety link and Enter; the rest is on the Safety page. Worlds are one sideways row of key art, most recently played first, with a + tile that opens the world packages. Without a text model, Send reads "Add a model" and opens Settings > Models instead of a banner.
- **Your Claude and ChatGPT accounts, found for you.** A Claude Code or Codex profile now lists the accounts on this
  PC to pick from: the usual one, plus each second command such as `claude2` (a script on PATH, or a function or
  alias in your PowerShell profile) and account folders like `.claude-account2`, with whether each is signed in and
  as whom. Codex profiles can now use a second ChatGPT account too, through their own account folder. (Ported from
  Prospero's Dungeon #234.)
- **Starting point:** a clean copy of [Prospero's Dungeon](https://github.com/vantaloomin/prosperos-dungeon)
  at main `a4663f1` (2026-11-02), renamed to Prospero's Adventure. It installs and runs beside Dungeon, the
  Companion and the Study: its own port (8805, dev interface 5197), data folder (`ProsperoAdventure`), credential
  service, backup format, Windows installer id and `ADVENTURE_` environment variables. World packages keep
  Dungeon's format, so Worldsmith worlds import unchanged. Dungeon's own history is in its repository.
- **Cleanup after the fork:** Dungeon's design notes (requirements, roadmap, Life Cards) are no longer copied here;
  code comments point to them in Dungeon's repository. The playtest runner stays, without Dungeon's single-player
  scenarios. The carry-over that kept Haddon Harbor for worlds made before it was retired is gone, since no
  Adventure world was ever set there.
