# Changelog

## Unreleased

- **A bank of characters for each world.** When a town has more leads than the table seats thinking characters (Havenbrooke's nine), they no longer all follow you. The cast page fills your party up to Thinking characters at once (6 by default) and puts the rest in a Bank row under it; a − on a party card sends them to the bank and a + brings one in. Whoever is still in the bank at Begin lives in town as a background character at their usual haunt (Piper at the Verdant Grove, Austin at Alpha Gamma House), and you can meet them and ask them along like anyone else.
- **Undo what the story's record keeper decided.** When the Continuity model reads a turn and decides a quest is over, an oath was sworn, someone's feelings shifted or your character did something dark, each one now shows in that turn's report under Read from the story, with Undo. Undo takes back only that change: an ended quest reopens with what it paid or settled, and a feeling moves back by what it moved, keeping what later turns did.
- **What you read, your character knows.** Facts the record keeper takes from a turn are always ones your character saw or was told, so they show in your journal. Only the game itself keeps secrets from you now.
- **The narrator gets signs, never secrets.** In every campaign, the narrator no longer gets the hidden truth behind a quest, a thread or someone carrying one, or facts hidden from you, except a lie it is telling this turn. It gets what each looks like on the page, and the truths stay in the GM view.
- **Author, Watch and Next turns count as story.** A turn with no line of yours now does what your own lines do once it is told: the record keeper reads it, so a quest can end there, an oath or a feeling can move (each in the turn report with Undo), and the summary keeps up. The Author's nudge is never read as something your character said. The game's director gives the Writer its story pressure in those turns too (what is due, a seed, room to breathe after a fight), and they count toward the pace, so a slack run of rounds brings pressure.
- **A thinking character can say no to joining you.** When you ask a character who thinks for themselves to come along, and they are not wary of you, they are asked whether they come and their answer stands, said aloud in their own words. A wary character still won't come however they are asked, and a full party still has no room. The question is under Settings > Developer > Prompts as Being asked along.
- **Thinking characters lie and leave for themselves.** A character who thinks for themselves lies to you only when they choose to, never because the game's rule for townsfolk says so. Sour feelings no longer make them walk out of your party; they leave only by their own move, after the signs come first. Feelings still decide whether one agrees to join.

- **Genre at the Table.** Under Tone and rules, Each kind on its own has a Genre beside Voice: none, dark romantasy, adventure or graphic violence. Each adds a short guide to how that genre is written to the narrator's and the round Writer's prompt, and each is editable under Settings > Developer > Prompts. The intensity still decides how far the page goes.
- **Name and delete story branches.** Under Other branches, each branch has Rename and Delete. Delete takes it out of the list and the chapter tree with Undo right there; nothing is erased, so branches taken from it keep what came before. The branch you are playing cannot be deleted. A new branch you go back to is named after the line it replaces (Instead of “I punch the bouncer”), and a branch that asked a character again keeps its question until you rename it. (Ported from Prospero's Companion #496.)
- **Your own picture as a portrait.** Under Pictures, Use my own picture (for you) and Use my own (for someone you met) take a PNG, JPEG or WebP of yours, shrunk to 2048 px in the browser. It is their portrait at once, marked added by you, never goes out of date, and becomes the likeness every later portrait of them follows on a backend that takes a reference picture. (Ported from Prospero's Companion #454.)
- **Pictures and models for each world.** With more than one world, Settings > Images can keep a world to the image backends you pick for it (Pictures in <world>), say an anime model in one world and photographs in another, and Settings > Models > Roles can give a world role assignments of its own (Models in <world>). Backends and profiles still follow you between worlds; the picks stay with each world. (Ported from Prospero's Companion #448.)
- **Other tabs follow a world switch at once.** Switching worlds in one tab tells this browser's other tabs, which reload into it straight away instead of at their next check. A page that followed a switch made in another tab or on another screen says so once over the story: "Now in Kingswood. You switched worlds on another screen, so this one came too." (Ported from Prospero's Companion #431.)
- **Check for updates.** Settings > General > Updates asks GitHub for the newest Adventure release (pre-releases count) when pressed, and says how to update this copy: the new installer, or update.bat / update.command for a folder copy. Nothing checks in the background, and nothing about you is sent. (Ported from Prospero's Companion #417.)
- **ComfyUI says which file it lacks.** When ComfyUI refuses a workflow, the picture error names the node and, for a missing model, the file and the models folder it belongs in, instead of "Prompt outputs failed validation". (Ported from Prospero's Companion #446.)
- **A saved model file stays in its list.** In Settings > Images, a model file, sampler, scheduler or style LoRA the ComfyUI server does not list yet stays pickable after you choose another, so you can go back to it without Use the default. (Ported from Prospero's Companion #468.)
- **The Sidebar Assistant.** Assistant in the navigation (or Sidebar Assistant in the story's menu, or Ask the assistant under a passage) opens a chat beside the story that sees the scene, the recent story, the people met, the journal, the lore and the author's note. It answers questions and proposes changes you apply one by one, each with Undo: the author's note, a narrator's passage in new words, a journal fact added or corrected, and a new lore entry. With the GM view on it also sees what is behind the story and can propose GM edits: how someone feels, and cutting, bringing back or adding an arc's planned beats. Nothing said in it reaches the narrator. It has its own job in Settings > Models. (Ported from Prospero's Companion's sidecar.)
- **No browser suggestions in text boxes.** Text and search boxes no longer offer words typed into other boxes. (Ported from Prospero's Companion #433.)
- **Italics and bold in the story.** Markdown the narrator writes (*italics*, **bold**, ~~struck~~, `code`) shows as styling instead of asterisks, in the story, the streaming narration, reply cards, turn reports and chapter text, and a line of *** or --- reads as a scene break. Names in bold or italics still link to their cards, and selecting styled text to Rewrite finds it. The reading and UI fonts now ship their italic and bold faces, so italic lines such as scene moves are italic too.
- **Character prompts in Settings.** Settings > Developer > Prompts has a Characters group: a character's mind, each moment they are asked (deciding a move, reacting, speaking up, answering, off the page) and turning on someone, each with Reset to default. The round Writer, which tells rounds when other characters think for themselves, joins the Story group. What each character may know and the shape of their answer stay with the app, so a rewording can't show anyone a secret.
- **Sharper prompts for adult fiction.** The narrator, the round Writer and every character now share one order of what wins when instructions disagree and one content scope: adults written as adults with no childlike framing, real people never in sexual content or harmed, and dark material written with full craft and no moralizing. The writing rules keep only checkable hard rules and state the rest as tendencies; the list of stock phrases moved out of the prompt into the checks that run after each reply, so it no longer primes the model. The Reviewer now checks over-explanation, repeated moves, questions and concrete detail as well, and reads a familiar phrase as a place to look rather than an automatic cut.

## v0.1.0 (2026-11-03): Characters Who Think

- **Play, Author and Watch on the cast page.** Beside the place name on the cast page, pick the mode the story starts in. In Author and Watch no card is You: the character whose story it is gets Thinks or Rules like the rest, for who plays them while you author or watch.
- **Your party on the cast page.** Once the game seats the other leads as your party at Begin, every lead but the one you play shows Thinks or Rules under their card (the card you play is marked You), and Begin sends how each one decides.
- **Notes and a model for each thinking character.** A thinking character's open row in the cast list has your notes on them and, when you have a finished model, which model they think with (Agents' model by default).
- **Ask a character again.** Open a thinking character's row in the cast list after a turn and Ask them again: the turn plays again on a new branch with only their choice made afresh. Under Branches, a branch made this way reads as a question with their portrait: "What if Hesk had chosen otherwise?"
- **Beats in Author and Play.** In Author, Beats under the nudge box opens the director's plan for each arc: add a beat, cut one or bring it back, and move beats up or down, with each marked Used or On the page as the story reaches it. In Play, Beat under your line adds a beat you would like to see (a storm, a stranger arriving), which the next turn takes into its brief; remove one with its ×.
- **Who knows a lore entry.** On the Lore screen each entry has Everyone, Some characters (tick the characters at the table who know it) or GM only, which decides whose turns read it.
- **Play, Author and Watch on the play screen.** Once a character thinks for themselves, the scene line has a Play, Author and Watch switch; a change made mid-turn counts from the next turn. In Author the message box takes a nudge ("Nudge the story"), Next with none plays the next turn as it comes, and a line under the box says whether the last nudge took. In Watch the box gives way to Every (the wait between turns, or By hand), Play / Pause and Next. Play goes on turn after turn until you pause or something stops it, and then one banner says why: a big moment ("Paused: Mara betrayed the party") with Continue and Switch to Author, the session's cap with Raise cap and Stop here, or a failed turn with Try again. Leaving Play the first time asks "Show the motives you missed?" (Keep hidden or Show) once the backend keeps that answer. Outside Play, a character's open row shows their motive. Each line of a round turn has its Report, and a line whose turn had the GM view open is marked with an eye.
- **Dungeon's systems with agents at the table.** An agent knows what its faction wants, what it is working toward now, its rivals and allies and how your character stands with it, never what the faction's clocks will bring. The lead proposes where the party goes (your character in Play, the party's leader in Author and Watch), and a party member on an agent who refuses to follow splits off where the party left them until the party comes back or they rejoin; going back undoes it. Lore timers count rounds in every mode, and Author and Watch lines open chapters like your own. Ideas in Author are nudges drawn from what the Author has seen, never hidden truths or motives missed in Play, and Watch has no Ideas. The book export can set each turn's motives and whispers beside its post; the default is the story only.
- **Signs come before a betrayal.** When a party member running on an agent turns against the party (their own motives speak of it, or their trust and affection toward your character have soured), the director gives the Writer a warning sign to show ("Hesk's purse is heavier than it should be"), never the secret behind it. A betrayal with no sign on an earlier turn is held back one turn, its sign placed instead, so every betrayal is fair in hindsight. A foe the director makes a nemesis gets an agent of its own in a campaign played with agents; demote it by hand and it stays demoted.
- **Author and Watch play, and switching between modes.** In Author, a nudge sent with Next goes to the director and the Writer as a hidden note, and the line under the nudge box says after the turn whether it took ("Used: Mara took it up.", "Not yet: no one took it up this turn."); the beat board is the director's plan for each arc, where the Author adds, cuts and reorders beats and sees which have landed. In Play, beats you would like to see (a storm, a stranger arriving) can wait beside your line and go to the director the same way. Watch plays turns on your timer and pauses at deaths, betrayals, chapter ends, a new nemesis and the session cap, with Continue to go on; Next steps by hand. Outside Play your character runs on an agent or its Program mind, your pick, and leaving Play asks whether to see the motives you missed, hidden unless you say so. (Backend; the screens come with the play screen work.)
- **The GM view asks first while characters think.** In Play with a character who thinks, opening the GM view asks "This shows every secret and motive. Open anyway?", with "Don't ask again in this story". Once open, the GM view lists each mind's intent, lines, whispers and motive for the turn. A campaign where nobody thinks opens it as before.
- **The last turn's report.** Between turns, under the round timeline, the last turn's number opens its report in place: what you set, each roll in plain words (with the number when rolls are shown), the motives the mode lets you see, what the story made up that turn (each with Strike, which takes it out and rewrites the passage), the checks' rewrites, open questions, corrections, and where the turn stopped.
- **One Writer for a round with characters who think.** When agents share the scene, the round is written once from everything it settled: what each character said and did, every roll as the story feels it (never the dice), the beats you set in full, and the cutaways to people off the page, stopping before your character's next real choice. In Play it stays close on your character; in Author and Watch it roves across the cast, and each Author or Watch turn now leaves its prose in the story. A campaign with no agents is told exactly as before.
- **Pace in Settings > Models.** Quick, Balanced or Rich for the characters who think, each with its calls and tokens a turn. Advanced holds how many think at once, exchanges a turn, how readily they scheme, how fast loyalties shift, and a token cap for the session with what it has spent so far.
- **Characters who think for themselves, on the play screen.** Open a character in who's here to choose Thinks (an agent) or Rules (Dungeon's code minds); a character who thinks has an accent dot on their portrait, and the round timeline (Declare, React, Talk, Roll, Write) sits above the cast, lighting and naming the step under way ("Declaring · 2 of 3"). A row says only that a character has decided, never what. When a character speaks to yours, the message box gives way to a reply card with their line and the two before it: Reply, Say nothing, or Let your character answer for that one line. The card comes back after a reload, the tab's title shows (1) while it waits, and screen readers hear "2 of 3 characters have decided". A campaign where nobody thinks looks and plays as before.
- **Lore that not everyone knows.** A lore entry can be public, known to some characters, or GM only, and the narrator reads only public lore. Untagged lore stays public. A world package's hidden truths and its cast's secrets count as GM only or known to the characters they belong to, never public, and the world importer names any visibility tag it cannot read.
- **Agents in Settings > Models.** A new Agents role picks the model the agent characters think with (the Default without one), and a character can have a model of its own. The agents' preset (Quick, Balanced, Rich, each with an estimate of calls and tokens a turn), how much they scheme, how fast their loyalties shift and a spend cap for the session are kept per campaign, ready for the cast and settings screens.
- **World pitches on hover.** Hovering or focusing a world on the title screen floats its pitch over the art in a frosted card above the name, which stays put.
- **Plainer model settings.** Models, Decisions and Recall are each a heading and an Add button, without the explanations above them. The computer report shows only when a chosen local model won't fit, and the job picker is called Roles.
- **A calmer play screen.** The scene is one line: the place (tap it to go somewhere else), the time and how you are. The composer is the message box and Send; Ideas, the author's note, stepping out of the story, every panel view and starting a new story are in a menu (the button beside the box, or ⌘K / Ctrl+K). The panel opens on who's here: you first, then the people met at this place, each opening to show more, with who else is around and the weather under them. The Scene, Sheet, Pack, Journal and More tabs are gone. "Characters are AI and can make mistakes" stays under the box.
- **One cast page.** "Who are you?" and "How this campaign plays" are one page: the town's people as a grid, tap one to play them and read who they are, or + to make your own; Rules, Tone (As written, Gentler, Harsher) and Begin at the foot. Ironman, an earlier story and how far you can rise sit behind Options; the finer tone, narration and dice choices start from the preset and your last campaign and change in play under Tone and rules.
- **A quieter start.** The first-run notice is one line over the title art ("Everyone here is AI"), the 18-or-older box, a Safety link and Enter; the rest is on the Safety page. Worlds are one sideways row of key art, most recently played first, with a + tile that opens the world packages. Without a text model, Send reads "Add a model" and opens Settings > Models instead of a banner.
- **Your Claude and ChatGPT accounts, found for you.** A Claude Code or Codex profile now lists the accounts on this PC to pick from: the usual one, plus each second command such as `claude2` (a script on PATH, or a function or alias in your PowerShell profile) and account folders like `.claude-account2`, with whether each is signed in and as whom. Codex profiles can now use a second ChatGPT account too, through their own account folder. (Ported from Prospero's Dungeon #234.)
- **Starting point:** a clean copy of [Prospero's Dungeon](https://github.com/vantaloomin/prosperos-dungeon) at main `a4663f1` (2026-11-02), renamed to Prospero's Adventure. It installs and runs beside Dungeon, the Companion and the Study: its own port (8805, dev interface 5197), data folder (`ProsperoAdventure`), credential service, backup format, Windows installer id and `ADVENTURE_` environment variables. World packages keep Dungeon's format, so Worldsmith worlds import unchanged. Dungeon's own history is in its repository.
- **Cleanup after the fork:** Dungeon's design notes (requirements, roadmap, Life Cards) are no longer copied here; code comments point to them in Dungeon's repository. The playtest runner stays, without Dungeon's single-player scenarios. The carry-over that kept Haddon Harbor for worlds made before it was retired is gone, since no Adventure world was ever set there.
